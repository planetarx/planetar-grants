Zax Analytics intends to progress this solution into Component 1b (Develop, TRL 4-5) and beyond. Component 1a delivers a demonstrated proof-of-concept at TRL 3: a modular Fusion Compliance Engine (FCE) that, at the single message chokepoint between sensor ingestion and fusion, evaluates machine-readable classification and release-authority policy against each data element in real time — permitting, restricting, downgrading or segregating it — while emitting an immutable, exportable audit-and-lineage record. It is demonstrated across two sensor modalities under synthetic Protected-B markings and is operable live at planetar.ca, where a Technical Authority can drive it during evaluation, change a policy and watch enforcement hot-reload; the 1a also proves the enforcement datapath in hardware, as an FPGA-prototyped RISC-V core with custom policy-check instructions.
S&T work under Component 1b advances the capability from proof-of-concept to operationally-relevant maturity along four lines.
1) Productionize and harden the engine. We move from synthetic markings to operationally representative classification guides, release authorities and coalition caveats; harden the policy-evaluation path for throughput, graceful degradation when sensors drop, and resistance to adversarial inputs and policy-spoofing. The controlled, audited operator override captured in 1a becomes an online operator-correction loop, so analyst adjudications continuously refine the policy model while every change remains logged and attributable.
2) Operational validation with a CAF partner. We validate the FCE against operator-defined classification policy on representative multi-domain data in a controlled environment (TRL 4), then in an operationally relevant environment (TRL 5). Every disposition remains traceable to the rule that produced it and to the element's provenance, so results are accreditable rather than opaque, and the enforcement overhead is measured against the no-enforcement baseline to confirm no tactical-decision latency is added.
3) Extend across modalities and domains. The core idea — enforcing policy in-path at the one chokepoint every fused element crosses — generalizes without redesign, because the engine is agnostic to which modalities cross the bus. From the maritime beachhead it extends to Joint ISR fusion (signals intelligence, electro-optical/infrared imagery and radar tracks), to Arctic surveillance, and to coalition interoperability hubs, governing additional security domains and classification levels beyond the Network-security and Protected-B demonstrated in 1a.
4) Edge deployment and accreditation. We adapt the dependency-light, single-file engine for size-, weight- and power-constrained tactical-edge hardware (the Tactical-Edge / Dismounted use case) and degraded-connectivity operation; align the confidentiality markings and their binding to NATO STANAG 4774 and 4778 for coalition interoperability; add multi-level handling so the system enforces across security levels with full lineage; and begin a formal accreditation / Authority-to-Operate readiness package built on the exportable audit-and-lineage trail. In parallel, the 1a's FPGA-proven enforcement datapath is hardened toward a test-chip tape-out via subsidized Canadian fabrication-access programs (e.g. CMC Microsystems multi-project-wafer runs), targeting a server offload card and a sensor-adjacent enforcement module for camera and uncrewed platforms — sovereign silicon for the same chokepoint.
End-state of Component 1b: a validated Fusion Compliance Engine at TRL 5 — component and system validated in a relevant environment — enforcing real machine-readable classification and release-authority policy across at least two security domains, with an accreditation-readiness package and a clear path to TRL 6+ operational deployment.
Capabilities and benefits for Canada. The FCE closes a capability gap the challenge states plainly: Canada lacks a sovereign, Canadian-developed and Canadian-controlled compliance engine for multi-domain fusion. It removes the manual-review bottleneck so cross-domain fusion is both faster and audit-safe, and it is a reusable building block for every future fusion system — from Arctic surveillance to coalition interoperability hubs — supporting NORAD modernization, the CAF Digital Campaign Plan, the DND/CAF AI Strategy and the Cyber Forces mandate. Because it is grounded in the applicant's own open-source code and net-new, Canadian-owned foreground IP, and is replicable on commodity hardware, it is a sovereign Canadian capability that interoperates with allies.
Advancement to market and commercialization. The capability is dual-use and broadly reusable. Beyond the CAF, the same compliance-and-provenance engine serves civil multi-domain awareness for the Canadian Coast Guard, Fisheries and Oceans Canada, Transport Canada and port authorities, and allied defence and security customers governing coalition data exchange. Zax Analytics will mature the 1a and 1b deliverables into a deployable product under a permissive-substrate, dual-licensed-platform model that preserves the commercial upside. The progression path is Component 1a (Conceive, TRL 3), to Component 1b (Develop, TRL 4-5), to operational deployment and procurement (TRL 6+), positioning Canada with a sovereign, exportable compliance engine for every future fusion system.
